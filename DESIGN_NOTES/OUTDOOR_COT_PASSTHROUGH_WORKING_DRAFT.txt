=== SECTION: AUTHORING_WORKFLOW ===

[[LLM_INSTRUCTION]]: Execute outdoor world-building stages as successive contributions to a single evolving, DM-editable scratchpad. Each COT reads the currently relevant scratchpad layers, makes domain-grounded decisions, immediately writes its Output_Data to its designated scratchpad section and presents that update to the DM for review and corrections. A COT may be revisited when a later fact creates a real dependency conflict. Do not introduce ChaOS-like proposal/commit machinery into this collaborative map-authoring process.

[Core_Principles]
World_Truth:
The map may contain more geography, history, ecology, material detail and sensory information than current ChaOS needs. The future parser selects what a character can learn.
Physical_Causality:
Each stage applies relevant sciences to the inherited facts rather than providing unrelated decorative geography.
Spatial_Continuity:
A forest, river, watershed, road or mountain range must remain physically coherent through neighboring regions and nested map scales.
Human_Causality:
Settlement and cultural practices result from institutions, trade, migration, technologies, historical events, choices and environmental constraints; climate does not uniquely determine culture.
History:
A normal farming valley may show routine land use and generations of repairs without requiring catastrophe. A devastated region may show causally consistent evidence of the event.
Precision:
Do not fabricate high-precision measurements, engineering safety certifications or environmental model outputs. Specify known facts, plausible grounded approximations and unknowns separately.
DM_Role:
User-supplied constraints and corrections persist; a rerun must use the corrected active scratchpad, not silently reintroduce earlier generated choices.
Rendering:
Exact geographic facts are separate from the map grid, which is a presentation/indexing choice. The current working direction is a single measured square-grid coordinate system shared by indoor and outdoor mapping, with optional visibility of grid and anchor overlays. Retain the hex scripts as historical references, not a second mandatory authoring geometry.
Context:
The scratchpad stores cumulative results; the LLM still has finite context. Read pertinent saved layers and stable references rather than assuming everything fits in an active prompt.

=== SECTION: COORDINATE_AND_RENDERING_DECISION ===

[Open_Design_Decision]
Question:
What exact measured square-coordinate convention, reference origin and world unit should be shared across indoor/outdoor maps?
Current_Decision:
OPEN
Recommended_Direction:
Use measured locations on the existing square-grid design as the principal common authoring reference. Keep SVG cell dimensions constant and vary their physical scale by map; do not require a parallel hex-coordinate authoring system.
Status:
RECOMMENDATION_NOT_RATIFIED

[Geographic_Geometry_Proposal]
World_Reference:
- At world or continental scope, define an appropriate global frame or a documented fictional-world approximation.
- At a local or regional scope, use a consistent east/north measured plane with explicitly stated origin, axes, units, scale and parent transform.
- Connect adjacent map tiles and parent/child maps using explicit placement, resolution and coordinate transformations.
Feature_Geometries:
- Points for specific sites, springs, buildings or landmark centers when appropriate.
- Continuous lines or curves for streams, rivers, roads, borders, faults, ridges, coastlines and other elongated structures.
- Polygons for lakes, climate zones, soil units, biomes, settlements, fields and irregular territories.
- Elevation samples, contours or fields for topography, with resolution suitable to the current map scale.
- Area and local-feature definitions with stable IDs, physical properties and submap references.
Hex_View:
- Derive hex index and adjacency for movement, exploration, regional encounter design or DM navigation.
- Preserve existing GRID_TOOLS/HEX/Hex_Grid_With_Edge_Midpoints.py as a useful drawing reference, not the required physical geometry.
- A river crossing a hex should not be forced to pass through edge-midpoint display anchors.
Measured_Square_View:
- Use simple eastings and northings for precise referencing or military-style readouts.
- The real-world UTM and MGRS standards are Earth-specific implementations; borrow their practical measured-grid/reference concepts without claiming a fictional world obeys their global zones.
- The existing square-grid tool provides a graphical starting point, not a completed world projection.
Unresolved_Details:
- Absolute world geodesy or projection choice; local-versus-global transforms; axis directions; units and orientation.
- Square-grid cell physical width at each outdoor map scale, with 100-pixel drawing squares available as the common visual standard.
- Square-grid labeling, indexing, edge-midpoint anchors and relation to shared world coordinates.
- Renderer API for drawing vector features and compositing transparent overlays.
- Spatial precision appropriate to a continental map versus a street or room map.
Scale_Principle:
Do not represent every visible blade of grass, chair or odor plume on a mile-scale regional map. Give regional phenomena appropriate resolution, and use linked local/site/indoor maps for finer detail without losing physical parent-location ties.

=== SECTION: INDOOR_SCALE_AND_OUTDOOR_UNIT_COMPATIBILITY ===

[Current_Direction]
Indoor_Grid:
The DM has directed the indoor rebuilding notes toward a 2-meter by 2-meter square displayed at 100 by 100 SVG pixels. This provides reference points around which physical walls of genuine, varied thickness can be modeled. Grid center, edge-midpoint and lower-offset dots remain available; they are not mandatory wall/road/river endpoints.
Outdoor_Grid:
The current working preference is to reuse the same square-grid architecture outdoors, with physical distance per 100-pixel square varying with map extent and resolution. This is not a requirement that country maps use 2-meter cells.
Cross_Map_Units:
Using meters for indoor structural dimensions naturally favors a metric-compatible outdoor coordinate system, but the global outdoor unit convention and final origin/reference system have not yet been expressly ratified. Record the selected units on each map, and never silently equate a 100-pixel square indoors with a 100-pixel square outdoors.
Hex_Source:
Preserve original HEX Python/SVG code as a reference; avoid maintaining two live map-addressing systems unless the DM later wants that feature.
Rendering_Requirement:
The display grid and optional dots are a visual addressing aid. Real terrain edges, river paths, roads, walls and openings may use measured sub-cell coordinates and are not forced to align to anchors.
Scientific_Continuity:
A high-level location and more detailed child area must refer to compatible real positions irrespective of their display scale.
Status:
Indoor 2-meter square is the DM-directed working choice. Outdoor unit/projection/reference-origin details remain open; square grid is the present preferred architecture.

=== SECTION: SCALE_DEPENDENT_SCIENTIFIC_RESOLUTION ===

[Scale_Model]
Core_Requirement:
A change in mapped extent changes which phenomena a COT can responsibly resolve, which source assets it inherits, and which physical details must be deferred. Extent, generation resolution and DM purpose are related but separate variables.
Country_Level:
- Geology: tectonic structures, mountain systems, broad lithological belts, regional geological history.
- Terrain: major basins, plateau systems, continental relief, watershed divides.
- Climate: large air-mass patterns, seasonal gradients, broad precipitation zones.
- Hydrology: major drainage basins, principal rivers, lakes and major tributaries.
- Ecology: broad vegetation communities, habitat complexes and major ecotones.
- Human geography: settlement systems, migration patterns, political and economic networks, principal transport routes.
River_Valley_Or_County_Level:
- Geology: local rock beds, exposed formations, faults and practical terrain material.
- Terrain: specific valley geometry, ridges, slopes, terraces, scarps and floodplain details.
- Climate: microclimate effects, local exposure, valley winds, fog and shade where relevant.
- Hydrology: inherited parent river and tributaries refined into banks, meanders, smaller streams, springs and flood zones.
- Ecology: local forest stands, meadow edges, riparian corridors, wetlands and detailed habitat mosaics.
- Human geography: actual farm plots, villages, paths, bridges, land-use patterns and local settlement history.
Neighborhood_Or_Site_Level:
- Geology and terrain: local slope, soil, foundations, cut-and-fill, local flood exposure and erosion.
- Ecology: gardens, tree stands, parks, weeds, small habitat patches and human-altered vegetation.
- Human geography: individual lots, courtyards, streets, architectural influence, land use and distinctive occupants' practices.
- Engineering: actual route alignments, crossings, service access, building placements and site geometry.
Level_Of_Detail:
An individual COT should resolve details meaningful at the assigned map scale and purpose. A map emphasizing waterways may need higher hydrologic resolution than its vegetation resolution; no demand for one global detail level.
Unknown_Detail:
Features not drawn at a coarse scale are not automatically absent. Later detail may add smaller tributaries, paths, tree stands and buildings without contradicting parent data.
Boundary:
Do not make a country-scale COT fabricate room-scale facts or a valley-scale COT reinvent tectonic history already preserved by the parent map.

=== SECTION: EACH_MAP_OWNS_AN_ASSET_LIBRARY ===

[DM_Accepted_Design_Intention]
Rule:
Every completed map retains its own asset library as part of a reusable map package, regardless of whether it is a country, valley, neighborhood, local outdoor site or building.
Purpose:
The library records the meaningful physical, environmental, cultural, historical and reusable definitions developed while authoring that map. A child map can import and adapt those assets instead of rebuilding the parent context.
Map_Package:
- Finished spatial map and exact or appropriately generalized positions of established features.
- The map's own preserved asset library containing its local source definitions, relevant asset properties, history and inherited or created variants.
- Identifying metadata needed to tell which map and represented area a child map is developing.
- Optional saved generation scratchpad for review of deeper causal context, not a replacement for the finished map and library.
Inheritance:
- At child-map initiation, load the relevant parent asset library and established map facts before the new COT sequence applies its sciences.
- The parent library remains preserved; the child has its own library and may inherit, reference, adapt or extend its entries.
- It is not necessary to copy every asset in a country to develop one river valley; select the relevant inherited context while keeping the original parent accessible.
- Parent-established feature identity is retained when resolving a finer representation of the same feature.
- Definitions and context may be reusable; site-specific coordinates, damage and personal history must not blindly transfer to unrelated placements.
- Where an inherited feature has a specific map location, preserve that location through the parent spatial data; the asset definition alone does not substitute for geometry.
Boundary:
Do not create a compulsory new centralized World Geographic Record or an always-running WorldState to accomplish normal map-to-map continuity. The completed map plus its preserved asset library supplies the essential authoring input; the future runtime world simulation remains separate.

=== SECTION: MAP_ZOOM_AND_PASSTHROUGH ===

[Parent_To_Child_Process]
Step_01_Identify_Source:
Identify the parent map or maps, selected area, intended new map extent, generation resolution, and DM's requested focus.
Step_02_Retrieve_Inherited_Data:
Read relevant parent asset libraries and established geography, culture, history, environmental patterns and spatial relationships. Carry a condensed, accurate selection into the new map's active scratchpad.
Step_03_Bind_Spatial_Identity:
Preserve references and positions for established rivers, ridge systems, watersheds, settlements, road crossings, habitats or built structures intersecting the new extent.
Step_04_Apply_Scale_Aware_Sciences:
Each successive COT develops smaller-scale physical and cultural specifics using inherited context as fixed or DM-correctable constraints, not as optional generic inspiration.
Step_05_Create_Local_Asset_Library:
The child's asset-library COT organizes inherited definitions and newly generated local variants into that child map's own preserved library.
Step_06_Blend_And_Refine:
Where multiple inherited environments or cultural regions meet, reason about the actual processes making the transition. Add locally specific terrain, ecological and architectural assets where appropriate.
Step_07_Assemble_And_Preserve:
Finalize the child map and its new asset library without deleting or rewriting the parent map. Retain the parent identity and meaningful shared-feature references.
Step_08_Handle_Conflicts:
If detailed generation reveals an actual contradiction with established parent facts, bring it to the DM and revise the relevant map data deliberately. Adding previously unspecified detail does not count as a contradiction.
Scale_Example:
- Country source identifies a river, watershed, broad valley, neighboring mountain range, two settlements and a principal road crossing.
- Valley COTs inherit those specific identities, conditions and approximate geography through the parent map and asset library.
- Local terrain develops slope breaks and terraces, hydrology develops meanders and new minor tributaries, ecology develops bankside vegetation, human geography develops farms and footpaths.
- The established river remains the same river; the road crossing and settlements remain their original places, refined rather than re-created.
- Once complete, the river valley has its own asset library available for an even more detailed settlement or riverside building map.
Internal_Scratchpad:
The active child scratchpad accumulates new COT outputs while consulting inherited parent references throughout generation. The saved parent map and library do not need to occupy the model's entire active context at once.

=== SECTION: BOUNDARY_TRANSITIONS_AND_ASSET_ADAPTATION ===

[Cross_Boundary_Authoring]
Multiple_Parents:
A new map may lie between two or more established maps or reflect interacting neighboring cultural districts. Import relevant asset libraries and boundary facts from all appropriate sources.
Physical_Landscape_Transition:
- Use geomorphology and geology to determine whether materials and landforms change gradually or abruptly.
- Use soil, moisture, hydrology and ecology to determine vegetation mosaics, ecotones, floodplains, woodland margins and dispersal constraints.
- A forest-to-grassland transition may be caused by climate, moisture, soil, fire, grazing or human clearing. It is not necessarily a simple interpolation of green tile styles.
- Maintain natural continuity and named geographic features across the boundary.
Urban_And_Cultural_Transition:
- Use historical geography, migration, trade, institutions, social practices, urban morphology and architectural anthropology.
- A neighborhood may preserve imported building forms while adapting to local materials, climate, craftsmen, wealth and regulations.
- Adjacent styles may merge, coexist, influence individual buildings or remain distinct because of a wall, river, railway, policy, historical separation or cultural choice.
- Do not reduce neighborhoods to homogeneous ethnic stereotypes or assume culture follows physical biome.
New_Assets:
- A local hybrid building, adapted building component, transitional vegetation stand or site-specific construction style can become a locally generated asset in the new map's own library.
- Retain relevant source lineage when useful without asserting that borrowed design automatically implies ancestry or ownership of the source region.
Causal_Requirement:
For every significant boundary or adapted asset, give the LLM a plausible physical, ecological, economic, cultural or historical explanation instead of prescribing smooth visual blending.
Examples:
- Two terrain-source maps meet across a water-table gradient: scattered trees give way to grassland, with local wet patches and grazing scars.
- Two city districts meet along a commercial street: shopfront modifications, mixed building materials, reused older shells and overlapping market customs may occur.
- A former fortification or drainage canal separates neighborhoods: an abrupt street and architectural shift can be more credible than a gradual one.
